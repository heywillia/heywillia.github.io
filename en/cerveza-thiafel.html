The problem

In a neighborhood brewery, the can is the only place where the brand appears in full.

In a bar's fridge, the can competes with twenty other labels at the same distance and under the same light. It has to say whose it is before saying what style it is, and carry the mandatory information —alcohol, IBU, ingredients, volume— without the front turning into a spec sheet.

The other part of the problem is scale: every so often a new variety comes out, sometimes a limited edition or a collaboration with another brewery. The system had to withstand six years of releases without becoming repetitive or losing the family.

02 · The brand

A logotype that holds up small and in a single ink.

Sans-serif type with carved terminals, the hop as an icon and two service lines: the founding year on top, “cerveza artesanal” below. On the can it's always white, and on the rest of the pieces it works in black or gold without touch-ups.

03 · The label system

Same structure on all of them: an animal in a suit at the center, the variety name below and the spec sheet on the side panel.

What changes from one can to the next is the color, the landscape and the character. What never changes is where each thing sits: logo on top, portrait in the middle, big name below, and the side with alcohol, IBU, style and ingredients in the same hierarchy. Unfolded, the label is a single continuous scene wrapping the can.

The eleven labels unfolded: the name always on top, the character at the center and the spec sheet on the right side panel.
04 · The complete family

Eleven cans designed between 2020 and 2026. Together they read as a series; apart, each one has its own world.

Drag to browse all eleven.
05 · Collaborations

Three cans were made with other brands: Shadow and Charipa with Cruda Crew, Potrera with Muss Bier. There the front carries both signatures at equal weight and the system yields just enough —the name's typography changes so the can reads as a release of its own and not just another variety.

06 · The can in its scene

For social media, each release is photographed inside the world of its label. The communication piece doesn't explain the beer: it stages it.

07 · In the fridge

The real test is in a brewery's freezer or on a bar table, among twenty other people's labels and with whatever light there is.

What it left me

A system is tested with release number eleven, not with the first.

The brand manual here was short and stayed in the logo. What sustained the identity for six years was a packaging decision: fix the structure and leave each variety's world free. Every new beer could be different without negotiating anything, because what makes it Thiafel isn't the color: it's where each thing sits.
