Home Market · Herrajes Los Halcones
01
Context

Same owners, two business units that coexist while following two different paths. One sells wholesale; the other, to anyone who wants to build their own furniture.

Home Market is distribution and marketplace: hardware, accessories and decor, with direct sales to the public through Mercado Libre, its own Tiendanube store and a street-front shop in Buenos Aires. Herrajes Los Halcones is the factory: own product, B2B sales, a different tone, a different buyer.

Every channel asks for its own format, its own size and its own language. The same piece doesn't work for a Mercado Libre listing, a store banner and a story. Multiply that by two brands and by every month of the year: that's the real volume of the work.

02 · The role

I don't deliver pieces. I sustain the whole cycle.

From deciding what gets communicated to reading how it worked. Without handing the project from hand to hand and without the client having to coordinate suppliers.

05
Teams

When the volume stopped fitting in one person, I built the team. And I stayed in charge of the judgment.

Adding people works if something holds the consistency together. Before delegating I wrote the system down: framing and color rules, templates per format, copy tone per brand and a calendar where every piece has a date, a channel and an owner.

With that, the team produces without consulting every decision and I direct instead of reviewing. The client doesn't notice the change of hands, which is exactly the point.

What it left me

Five years with the same client aren't won with a campaign. They're won by holding the rhythm.

What I learned most here is that content is an operation, not a deliverable. The hard part isn't the good-looking piece: it's having a piece every week, in the right channel, with the same judgment, while the catalog changes and the platforms change with it.
